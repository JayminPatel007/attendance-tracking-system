# BSS Membership Is Additive, Not Replacement

When a child is selected for Bal Sevak Sabha (BSS), they gain a *second* demographic Home Sabha in addition to their Regular Baal/Balika Sabha — they do not move out of Regular. So a BSS-selected boy has Home Sabhas in both `(Baal, Regular)` and `(Baal, BSS)`, plus `(Sanyukta, Regular)`. The Home-Sabha-per-Sabha-kind model from ADR-implied Q18 extends naturally: "kind" includes the track dimension, and a Person has one Home Sabha per `(demographic, track)` pair they participate in.

## Why additive and not replacement

If BSS replaced Regular for selected children, the Regular Baal Sabha's attendance roll-up would systematically exclude the most committed children in the cohort. That introduces a bias that flows up through every Kshetra / Zone / City roll-up — the Regular numbers would look artificially worse than reality, while BSS numbers would look artificially better. Keeping selected children counted in both rolls keeps each Sabha's analytics honest about who is expected and who showed up.

## Consequences

- A Person can have an arbitrary number of demographic Home Sabhas (typically 1–2: Regular + optional BSS), plus a Sanyukta.
- BSS selection is an event on the Person — adds a Home Sabha, doesn't remove one. Deselection is the inverse.
- BSS Sabhas need their own **Sanchalak and Sah-Sanchalak** per `(Kshetra, demographic, BSS)`. The **Nirdeshak, Sah-Nirdeshak, Sanyojak, and Regional Team are shared with the Regular track** for the same demographic — the track dimension only differentiates the Sanchalak tier. **No Nirikshak exists for BSS**: the BSS Sanchalak reports directly to the demographic Nirdeshak. The same applies to YSS at the youth tier.

## Why higher tiers are track-shared

A naive read of "BSS is parallel to Regular" would suggest fully parallel role hierarchies — distinct Nirdeshaks, Sanyojaks, etc. for each track. In practice the organisation operates the *opposite* way at the higher tiers: a Baal Nirdeshak owns *all* Baal Sabhas in the Kshetra (Regular and BSS), because the selection decision (who gets nominated, who gets approved) is itself an act of leadership over the whole Baal cohort. Splitting the tier would create two competing decision-makers for one cohort. Splitting only at the Sanchalak tier matches reality: the Sanchalak runs the day-of session for *their* roster, and BSS rosters genuinely warrant a separate session-runner.

## Why no Nirikshak for BSS

The Nirikshak tier exists to balance load between Sanchalak and Nirdeshak when the Nirdeshak oversees many Sabhas. BSS rosters are deliberately small (selective program), so a BSS Nirikshak would be supervising 1–2 Sabhas per Kshetra — not enough load to justify the role. The demographic Nirdeshak absorbs the small BSS oversight directly.
